# Business Understanding

**Status: Complete**

## Business Context

Employee satisfaction can vary across departments, compensation levels, locations, work arrangements, and time. Leadership needs to identify where lower scores or recurring concerns are concentrated before deciding what to investigate or change.

## Main Business Question

> Where are the largest employee-satisfaction differences, which workplace concerns accompany them, and where should leadership investigate first?

## Business Problem

Company-wide averages can hide department-specific issues, while raw concern counts can overstate problems in larger departments. The analysis must compare groups fairly, connect survey results with workforce attributes, and distinguish association from cause.

## Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Decision supported |
|---|---|
| HR leadership | Prioritize employee-listening and engagement work |
| Department leaders | Investigate department-specific satisfaction gaps |
| Compensation team | Review bonus-related patterns |
| Workforce planning team | Compare location and work-arrangement context |
| Executive leadership | Monitor satisfaction trends and resource priorities |

## Business Objectives

1. Compare average satisfaction across departments.
2. Normalize concern frequencies so departments of different sizes can be compared.
3. Examine the association between bonus percentage and bonus satisfaction.
4. Identify geographic concentrations that merit further review.
5. Monitor satisfaction over time.

## Success Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Average satisfaction | Mean response score | Compare groups and trends |
| Department concern share | Concern responses divided by all department responses | Compare unequal department sizes |
| Bonus relationship | Pearson correlation with bonus satisfaction | Identify compensation patterns for investigation |
| Response count | Survey records in context | Assess sample size |

## Assumptions and Limitations

- The data is synthetic and demonstrates analytical technique only.
- A survey response, not an employee, is the primary analytical unit for satisfaction trends.
- Employees may submit multiple responses.
- Associations with bonus, location, or season do not establish causality.
- Geographic results may be confounded by department and work arrangement.

## Main Limitation

> The dashboard identifies where leadership should ask better questions; it does not prove why satisfaction differs or which policy will improve it.
